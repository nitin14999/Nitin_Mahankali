The problem

Conventional Haber–Bosch plants are huge, run continuously, and use catalysts that heat up and cool down slowly. Renewable electricity fluctuates, so a decentralized green-ammonia plant has to start, stop and change load often.

INP needed a containerized pilot plant to test this: one that is safe with hydrogen and pressurized ammonia, fits a real test field, and is instrumented well enough to show how a fast-response catalyst behaves under dynamic operation. There was little prior operating data to build on.

Role

Design engineer for the test field and experimentalist on the synthesis loop. Responsible for the layout, P&IDs, equipment and instrumentation, safety measures and container integration, working closely with civil engineers, suppliers and the safety team. Currently preparing the dynamic experimental campaign and developing equipment and plant-level models.

Plant capacity, operating data and drawings are confidential. This page shows scope and methods. Experimental results will follow once the campaign has run.

Key engineering decisions

Pressure-liquefied NH₃ storage

Ammonia is stored as a liquid under its own vapour pressure. At pilot scale this is simpler and needs less infrastructure than refrigerated storage, and it matches how the ammonia is handled downstream.

Layout built around access

Pipe and cable routes were laid out to keep forklift lanes clear. In a test field, containers and equipment get moved and exchanged, so access was a design requirement and not left until afterwards.

DOE-based test planning

The planned operating points are derived from the reactor and catalyst limits. A designed experiment will show main effects and interactions with far fewer runs, and every run will also serve as model-validation data.

Planned dynamic Haber–Bosch campaign

The catalyst heats up and cools down much faster than conventional ammonia-synthesis catalysts, which is what makes flexible operation possible. The campaign now in preparation is designed to measure exactly this dynamic behaviour.

The main challenge: very little data to start from

ChallengeThere was almost no operating data for this reactor and catalyst under dynamic conditions. Initially, only the temperature development could be observed.
RiskUnstructured testing would take too long and produce data that can't be compared or used for a model.
ApproachGas-tightness and N₂ supply secured first. Operating windows being defined from the reactor and catalyst limits, with a DOE plan to cut the number of runs and dedicated start-up and shutdown tests.
GoalA structured dataset for every operating point, covering conversion, temperature profiles and energy, that can feed directly into the plant model.

From equipment models to a plant model (in progress)

Each piece of equipment is modelled separately (mass and energy balances, plus dynamic behaviour where it matters). The equipment models are being connected into one plant-level model, which will be validated against pilot-plant data once the campaign has run.
